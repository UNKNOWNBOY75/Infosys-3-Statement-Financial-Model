# Infosys 3-Statement Financial Model

A beginner-friendly three-statement financial model for Infosys using historical FY2025-FY2026 financials and FY2027-FY2029 forecasts.

## 📊 Project Overview

This project builds an integrated financial model for Infosys Limited using:

- Historical Income Statement
- Historical Balance Sheet
- Historical Cash Flow Statement
- FY2027-FY2029 financial forecasts
- Key financial ratios
- Model checks
- Supporting financial analysis and charts

## 🧾 Three-Statement Model

The model integrates:

1. **Income Statement** – Revenue, expenses, profit before tax, taxes, and profit for the year.
2. **Balance Sheet** – Assets, liabilities, and equity.
3. **Cash Flow Statement** – Operating, investing, and financing cash flows.

The three statements are linked to create a consistent forecast model.

## 📈 Forecast Period

The forecast covers:

- FY2027
- FY2028
- FY2029

Key assumptions include:

- Revenue growth
- Other income as a percentage of revenue
- Employee expenses as a percentage of revenue
- Other operating expenses as a percentage of revenue
- Depreciation as a percentage of revenue
- Finance cost as a percentage of revenue
- Tax rate
- Capital expenditure as a percentage of revenue
- Trade receivables as a percentage of revenue
- Trade payables as a percentage of revenue
- Dividend payout ratio

## 📐 Financial Analysis

The model calculates key financial metrics including:

- Revenue Growth
- Net Profit Margin
- Operating Margin
- Return on Equity (ROE)
- Return on Assets (ROA)
- Current Ratio
- Debt-to-Equity
- CFO / Net Profit
- Free Cash Flow
- FCF Margin
- Dividend Payout Ratio
- Ending Cash

## ✅ Model Checks

The model includes checks for:

- Balance Sheet balancing
- Cash Flow consistency
- Forecast revenue growth
- Forecast dividend payout ratio

## 📊 Supporting Analysis

The workbook includes charts showing:

- Revenue Growth
- Profitability Forecast
- Free Cash Flow and Ending Cash

## 🛠️ Tools Used

- Microsoft Excel
- Financial Modelling
- Financial Statement Analysis
- Ratio Analysis

## ⚠️ Model Limitations

- Forecasts are model estimates and not management guidance.
- Forecast assumptions remain constant across the forecast period.
- Several Balance Sheet and Cash Flow items use historical ratios or simplified assumptions.
- Detailed taxes, working-capital drivers, acquisitions, buybacks, and investment flows are simplified.
